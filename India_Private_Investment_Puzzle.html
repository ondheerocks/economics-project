The Broken Keynesian Multiplier
Theory: Multiplier (k) = 1 / (1 − MPC). Massive public infrastructure spending should cascade into a broad consumption & investment boom.
Reality: The multiplier is severely dampened by three key domestic leakages.
Leakage 1 — Debt
Household debt surged to a record 41.3% of GDP.

Income is leaking into EMI payments before reaching the real economy.
Leakage 2 — Savings
Net household financial savings collapsed to a decadal low of ~5.3% of GDP.

Households are borrowing to consume, not to invest.
Leakage 3 — Imports
~80% of solar equipment & majority of semiconductor components are imported.

Multiplier leaks abroad rather than circulating domestically.
Net result: Even as public Capex soars, the demand signal reaching firms is far weaker than the headline spending figure implies.

Supply-Side Interventions & PLI
Theory — Supply-Side Economics & Market Failure
When the free market fails to clear the hurdle rate for critical technologies, the State acts as a "risk absorber" — artificially boosting MEC to crowd-in private investment.
The Policy Anchor: Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme
  • Total Outlay: ₹1.91 lakh crore across 14 strategic sectors
  • Mechanism: Moves from input subsidies → links financial rewards directly to incremental sales & production
  • Effect on MEC: Artificially raises the effective return on new capacity, making the Go / No-Go calculus viable in critical tech sectors
  • Target sectors: Semiconductors · EVs · Batteries · Specialty Chemicals · Pharma · Mobile Phones · White Goods · Solar PV · Textiles
Source: DPIIT PLI Annual Report FY25; Ministry of Finance Budget Documents

Corporate Balance Sheets vs. Crowding Out
"Twin Balance Sheet Advantage"
Unlike the 2010s (NPA crisis era), corporate India is highly deleveraged with record profitability. Internal financial ability to invest is exceptionally strong.
IS-LM Theory — Crowding Out Mechanism
  1. Massive government infra spending shifts IS curve outward →
  2. Sovereign borrowing absorbs domestic savings →
  3. Upward pressure on equilibrium interest rates →
  4. "Crowding out" private investment

r* ↑ → MEC of marginal projects no longer clears hurdle → deferral

India's Reality — Partial Crowding Out

Large corporates (immune):

  • Record profit retention funds capex internally
  • Access to corporate bond markets & private credit

MSMEs face the full effect:

  • Dependent on commercial bank lending
  • Cost of debt remains above their MEC
  • Expansions are being delayed

Net verdict: Crowding out is real but sector-specific.

The Energy-Digital Mismatch
Theory — Complementary Capital
To sustainably shift LRAS rightward via high-tech manufacturing, proportionate expansion in energy infrastructure is essential.
The Challenge
"New Economy" anchors ($11 billion fabs & AI data centers) are massively energy-intensive. Without a synchronized power boom, operational costs rise — destroying MEC.
Policy Response — Energy Security Roadmap
SHANTI Act 2025
₹20,000 crore Nuclear Energy Mission for Small Modular Reactors (SMRs).
Target: 100 GW nuclear capacity by 2047. Low-carbon baseload for fabs & data centres.
CCUS Initiative
₹20,000 crore for Carbon Capture & Utilisation infrastructure in heavy industries.
Focus: Green steel production — makes India's steel export-competitive.
Renewable Push
450 GW renewable target by 2030. Solar + storage to power EV charging networks.
Adani Green, NTPC, and Torrent Power commissioning gigawatt-scale projects.

The Final Verdict
Is Private Investment Still Not Picking Up?
NO — It is NOT structurally stagnant. The economy is selectively booming while traditional sectors exercise rational managerial discipline.
🟢 "New Economy" IS BOOMING
  • Sectors: High-tech, EV, Renewables, Semiconductors
  • MEC Range: 15%–25% → clears 3.55% hurdle easily
  • PLI scheme acts as government risk-absorber, making marginal projects commercially viable
  • FDI + China+1 supply chain integration providing additional demand certainty
  • Greenfield investments proceeding at full pace
🔴 "Old Economy" IS WAITING
  • Sectors: FMCG, Steel, Textiles, Core Infra
  • MEC Range: 8%–10% → borderline viable
  • Trapped by 74.3% capacity utilization — below the 78–80% trigger threshold
  • Broken domestic multiplier: household debt at 41.3% of GDP, rural wages subdued
  • Rational deferral — not corporate failure

Trigger 2 — Interest Rates:

Action Plan for Corporate Planners

Action 1 — Stop evaluating purely for domestic consumption demand.

Highest ROIs are currently found by integrating domestic plants into China+1 global supply chains.

Action 2 — Prepare for the Turn.

Corporate balance sheets are at a decadal best. Firms possess the capital — they are simply stationed on the launchpad awaiting the final macroeconomic demand signal.
Conclusion: India's investment story is not broken — it is bifurcated.
The triggers to unlock broad-based private capex are identifiable, measurable, and closer than the headlines suggest.
